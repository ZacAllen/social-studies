Lenin died in January 1924. When the Congress of Soviets met that month, its first sitting was devoted to his memory, and Joseph Stalin, one of the Bolshevik leaders, vowed in the party's name to carry on his work [^death].

In May 1924 Stalin published a set of lectures as *The Foundations of Leninism* [^cql]. He promised no full survey, only a synopsis of what was "distinctive and new" in Lenin's contribution to Marxism [^intro]. His definition:

> "Leninism is Marxism of the era of imperialism and the proletarian revolution." [^intro]

Lenin, he argued, did not merely apply Marxism to Russia: he developed it further, under new conditions [^intro].

---

Stalin began from Lenin's analysis of **imperialism**: capitalism in its stage of monopoly trusts, banks and a 'financial oligarchy', carving up the world [^ch1]. It sharpens three contradictions: labor against capital, imperialist powers against each other, and the "civilised" nations against the hundreds of millions of colonial and dependent peoples [^ch1].

Why did Leninism arise in Russia? Because, Stalin answered, "Russia was the focus of all these contradictions of imperialism" [^ch1]. Under the Tsar, capitalist exploitation was fused with autocracy, with the oppression of non-Russian peoples, and with service as a reservoir of soldiers and profits for Western imperialism [^ch1].

---

The parties of the **Second International**, the pre-1914 federation of socialist parties, had expected revolution first where industry was most developed. Leninism answered that capitalism had become a world system. National economies were now "links in a single chain called world economy" [^ch3]. So:

> "the proletarian revolution is the result of the breaking of the chain of the world imperialist front at its weakest link" [^ch3]

In 1917 the chain broke in Russia, though Russia was less developed than France, Germany, Britain or America. Next time, Stalin suggested, it might break in India, where a young proletariat had the national liberation movement as its ally [^ch3].

---

What state comes after the revolution? Stalin followed Lenin: the **dictatorship of the proletariat**, the rule of the working class over the overthrown exploiters, to crush their resistance and carry the revolution through to socialism [^ch4]. It cannot be democracy "for the rich as well as for the poor" [^ch4]. It is democracy for the working majority and dictatorship against the bourgeoisie.

Its form is the **Soviets**, councils of workers, peasants and soldiers that join lawmaking and administration and teach working people to govern [^ch4]:

> "The Paris Commune was the embryo of this form; Soviet power is its development and culmination." [^ch4]

(The Paris Commune was the short-lived workers' government of Paris.)

---

A revolution in a peasant country needs allies. In the Western bourgeois revolutions, the peasantry had followed the liberal bourgeoisie. Leninism set out to turn it "into a reserve of the proletariat, into its ally" [^ch5].

The same logic applied to the colonies. Stalin attacked the leaders of the Second International, who "hesitated to put white and black, 'civilised' and 'uncivilised' on the same plane" [^ch6]. For Leninism, the oppressed nations of Asia and Africa were allies in the fight against imperialism, and the national question could be solved only "in connection with and on the basis of the proletarian revolution" [^ch6].

---

To lead all these forces, the working class needs a party of a new type:

> "The Party is the General Staff of the proletariat." [^ch8]

It is the "highest form of class organisation of the proletariat", bound by iron discipline and allowing no factions [^ch8]. Stalin also named the style a Leninist should work in: "Russian revolutionary sweep" combined with "American efficiency" [^ch9]. Sweep without efficiency, he warned, decays into empty scheme-making; efficiency without sweep decays into narrow practicalism [^ch9].

---

**The debate:** Could socialism be built in one country? The May 1924 edition said no. Leon Trotsky, Stalin's opponent, quoted it back at him in 1928: "Can this task be accomplished, can the final victory of socialism in one country be attained, without the joint efforts of the proletariat of several advanced countries? No, this is impossible" [^tro2].

Stalin had corrected it in December 1924 [^cql]. The revised text holds that the victorious country "can and must build a socialist society" [^ch3]. Stalin called the first wording "obviously inadequate, and therefore incorrect" [^cql]. Trotsky answered:

> "The productive forces are incompatible with national boundaries." [^tro3]
