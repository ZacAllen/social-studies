Vladimir Ilyich Ulyanov was born in Simbirsk, on the Volga River, in 1870. In 1887 the Tsar's government executed his older brother Alexander for plotting to kill the Tsar [^tl].

Vladimir became a Marxist. He was arrested in 1895 and exiled to Siberia, and in 1901 he first signed himself 'Lenin' [^tl]. In 1903 the Russian Social-Democratic Labor Party (the Marxist party) split into two factions, the **Bolsheviks** led by Lenin and the **Mensheviks** [^tl]. His pamphlet *What Is to Be Done?* (1902) had already argued for a disciplined party of professional revolutionaries.

---

The First World War began in 1914, and Lenin was forced to leave for Switzerland [^tl]. There he wrote *Imperialism, the Highest Stage of Capitalism*, his account of the economics behind the war.

In February 1917 the Tsar abdicated and a **Provisional Government** took over. Lenin reached Petrograd in April and published his *April Theses*, calling for that government's overthrow [^tl]. Power, he argued, belonged to the **Soviets** (councils elected by workers, soldiers and peasants):

> "Not a parliamentary republic—to return to a parliamentary republic from the Soviets of Workers' Deputies would be a retrograde step—but a republic of Soviets" [^april]

---

After the 'July Days' uprising, Lenin went into hiding in Finland. He came back to Petrograd secretly in mid-October and pressed for an immediate insurrection, despite strong opposition inside the party [^tl]. In August and September he had written *The State and Revolution*, on what a workers' revolution must do with the old state [^srpost].

On October 25, 1917 (November 7 by the modern calendar), the Soviet government was formed with Lenin as its chairman [^tl]. His proclamation that morning began:

> "The Provisional Government has been deposed." [^citizens]

It promised "the immediate offer of a democratic peace, the abolition of landed proprietorship, workers' control over production, and the establishment of Soviet power" [^citizens].

---

The next day, the Second All-Russia Congress of Soviets passed Lenin's first decrees. The Decree on Land read:

> "Landed proprietorship is abolished forthwith without any compensation." [^land]

The Decree on Peace called on all the warring peoples to start talks at once for "a just, democratic peace", which Lenin defined as "a peace without annexations and indemnities" [^peace]. In March 1918 the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk ended the war with Germany [^tl].

In January 1918 the Bolsheviks dissolved the newly elected **Constituent Assembly** (a parliament to write a constitution), and power stayed with the Soviets [^tl]. In March 1919 Lenin founded the **Communist International**, to unite revolutionary parties across the world [^tl] [^glos].

---

The revolution had to defend itself in a civil war that lasted until 1920 [^nep], while the Allied powers blockaded the country [^famine]. In August 1918 Fanny Kaplan shot and wounded Lenin [^tl].

The Soviet state answered its enemies with terror, carried out by its political police, the **Cheka**. Lenin defended it as self-defense:

> "We were forced to use terror in response to the terror employed by the Entente" [^cec]

The Entente were Britain, France and their allies, who sent armies against the Soviets [^cec]. Estimates of the Cheka's executions run from its own count of 12,733 to 140,000 (the historian George Leggett) and 200,000 (Robert Conquest) [^mawdsley]. In February 1920 Lenin backed a proposal to abolish "the passing of all death sentences by the Cheka" [^cec].

---

Drought, years of war, the blockade and the forced requisition of grain caused the famine of 1921–1922. Estimates of the dead run from 1 million to 10 million, most often given as 5 million [^famine].

In early 1921 sailors at the Kronstadt fortress rose against the government [^tl]. Lenin admitted that "they wanted to correct the Bolsheviks in regard to restrictions in trade" [^tenth]. Red Army troops, with party congress delegates, stormed it across thin ice, where many drowned [^short]. That March the party adopted the **New Economic Policy** (NEP) [^tl], which Lenin called "a strategical retreat" [^nep]. He named the error:

> "we made the mistake of deciding to go over directly to communist production and distribution" [^nep]

Lenin died on January 21, 1924 [^glos].

---

**The debate:** The Communist leader Rosa Luxemburg wrote *The Russian Revolution* in prison in 1918 [^publicist]. She praised the Bolsheviks, but warned against ruling by one party alone:

> "Freedom only for the supporters of the government, only for the members of one party – however numerous they may be – is no freedom at all." [^luxrr]

In 1922 Lenin replied that she had been mistaken on several questions, including what she wrote in prison, and had corrected most of those errors after her release. Yet he still honored her: "But in spite of her mistakes she was—and remains for us—an eagle." [^publicist]
