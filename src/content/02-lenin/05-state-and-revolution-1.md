Russia was in the middle of a revolution. In August and September 1917, on the eve of the October Revolution, Lenin wrote *The State and Revolution* [^post].

The question was urgent. The world war had made the state, merged with giant capitalist firms, ever more oppressive, and Lenin saw revolution coming: "The world proletarian revolution is clearly maturing." [^pref]

Yet most socialist leaders had forgotten what Marx and Engels taught about the state. Lenin's aim was to restore that teaching, and to explain to the masses "what they will have to do before long to free themselves from capitalist tyranny" [^pref].

---

What is the state? Lenin began with Friedrich Engels, Marx's lifelong collaborator. The state did not always exist. It arose when society split into classes whose interests could never be brought into harmony [^ch1]. Lenin drew the conclusion:

> "The state is a product and a manifestation of the irreconcilability of class antagonisms." [^ch1]

So the state is not a referee standing between the classes. It is, in Marx's view as Lenin put it, "an organ of class rule, an organ for the oppression of one class by another" [^ch1].

---

What does the power of the state consist of? Lenin answered bluntly: "special bodies of armed men having prisons, etc." [^ch1], meaning the police and the standing army.

Why does society need armed men placed above it? Because the classes are irreconcilable. If everyone were armed equally, the classes would fight it out. So the ruling class keeps its own armed force [^ch1].

Every revolution, Lenin wrote, shows the naked class struggle. The ruling class tries to restore its armed bodies, and the oppressed class tries to build new ones that will serve "the exploited instead of the exploiters" [^ch1].

---

Does a democratic republic change this? Lenin agreed it was the best form of state for the workers' struggle, but not a neutral one. Wealth rules through it by corrupting officials and by the alliance of government with the stock exchange [^ch1]:

> "A democratic republic is the best possible political shell for capitalism, and, therefore, once capital has gained possession of this very best shell … it establishes its power so securely, so firmly, that no change of persons, institutions or parties in the bourgeois-democratic republic can shake it." [^ch1]

---

Engels wrote that the state 'withers away'. Reformists took this to mean that capitalism's state would fade quietly, without revolution. Lenin showed that Engels meant something else. The bourgeois state is abolished by the revolution; what withers away later is the workers' own state, the "proletarian state or semi-state" [^ch1].

Marx drew the same lesson from the French revolutions of 1848–51. In his words, quoted by Lenin, earlier revolutions had strengthened the state machine: "All revolutions perfected this machine instead of smashing it." [^ch2] The task now was to break it [^ch2].

---

What replaces the old state? Marx's answer was the **dictatorship of the proletariat**: the working class organized as the ruling class, holding down the old exploiters until classes disappear. In a section added in 1918 [^pref], Lenin made this the test of a Marxist:

> "Only he is a Marxist who extends the recognition of the class struggle to the recognition of the dictatorship of the proletariat." [^ch2]

Such a state, he wrote, would be "democratic in a new way (for the proletariat and the propertyless in general) and dictatorial in a new way (against the bourgeoisie)" [^ch2].
