In the spring of 1916 Lenin was living in exile in Zurich. There he wrote a short "popular outline" of the newest stage of capitalism [^pref1].

He wrote it to pass the Tsar's censors. So he kept to economics and made his political points by hints, "in that accursed Aesopian language" [^pref1] (the language of fables). Many socialists had turned into what he called social-chauvinists, "socialism in words, chauvinism in deeds" [^pref1]. Lenin set out to prove that the war of 1914–18 was imperialist on both sides:

> "it was a war for the division of the world, for the partition and repartition of colonies and spheres of influence of finance capital" [^pref2]

---

Lenin began with production. Large firms swallow small ones. In Germany in 1907, 586 giant establishments employed nearly a tenth of all industrial workers and used almost a third of all steam and electric power [^ch1].

Past a certain point, a handful of firms can divide the market among themselves through **cartels** and **trusts** (agreements and mergers that fix prices and share out markets). Lenin put the result in five words:

> "Competition becomes transformed into monopoly." [^ch1]

Capitalism had begun as free competition. Its own development now turned it into its opposite.

---

The banks went through the same change. Lenin wrote that the banks "grow from modest middlemen into powerful monopolies" [^ch2], commanding almost all the money capital of a country.

Bank capital then merges with the capital of industrial monopolies. Lenin called this fusion **finance capital**, and the few men who control it a **financial oligarchy** (rule by a few) [^ch7].

---

What does finance capital do with the profits it cannot invest at home? Capitalism could raise the living standards of the masses, but then, Lenin wrote, "it would not be capitalism" [^ch4]. Instead, surplus capital goes abroad, where land, labor and raw materials are cheap and profits high:

> "Typical of the old capitalism, when free competition held undivided sway, was the export of goods. Typical of the latest stage of capitalism, when monopolies rule, is the export of capital." [^ch4]

Before the war, Lenin counted, capital exports brought the richest states an income of eight to ten thousand million francs a year [^pref2].

---

The great powers raced to seize colonies. After 1876 the colonies of the six biggest powers grew from 40 million to 65 million square kilometers [^ch6]. By the start of the twentieth century there was nothing left to take:

> "For the first time the world is completely divided up, so that in the future only redivision is possible" [^ch6]

That is why Lenin's briefest definition of imperialism was "the monopoly stage of capitalism" [^ch7]. Once the world is divided, a rising power can grow only by taking territory from another, and that means war.

---

Why did so many socialist leaders support the war? Lenin found the answer in the profits of empire. A handful of rich states draw "superprofits" from the colonies, over and above what they squeeze from their own workers:

> "it is possible to bribe the labour leaders and the upper stratum of the labour aristocracy" [^pref2]

This **labor aristocracy** became, in his words, the "labour lieutenants of the capitalist class" [^pref2], the base of reformism inside the movement. But imperialism could not last. Lenin concluded: "Imperialism is the eve of the social revolution of the proletariat." [^pref2]

---

**The debate:** Karl Kautsky, the leading theorist of the Second International (the pre-1914 federation of socialist parties), saw imperialism as a policy, not a stage of capitalism. In 1914 he suggested that the war might end in a cartel of the great powers:

> "the result of the World War between the great imperialist powers may be a federation of the strongest, who renounce their arms race." [^kautsky]

He called this possible phase **ultra-imperialism**. Lenin answered that it was a fable that hid the real contradictions. Kautsky's idea, he wrote, implied that the rule of finance capital "lessens the unevenness and contradictions inherent in the world economy, whereas in reality it increases them" [^ch7].
