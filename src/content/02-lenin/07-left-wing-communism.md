By 1920 the Bolsheviks had held power in Russia for two and a half years [^ch2]. In March 1919 they had founded the **Communist International** (the Comintern), to unite the new communist parties of the world [^tl].

Many of those parties were young and impatient. In Germany and elsewhere, 'Left' communists refused to work in trade unions or parliaments [^ch6]. Lenin wrote this pamphlet to warn them that such purity was a childhood illness, an 'infantile disorder', of the movement. He began from Russia's experience, which, he argued, had a significance "that is not local, or peculiarly national, or Russian alone, but international" [^ch1].

---

What was the secret of Bolshevik success? Lenin answered:

> "the Bolsheviks could not have retained power for two and a half months, let alone two and a half years, without the most rigorous and truly iron discipline in our Party, or without the fullest and unreserved support from the entire mass of the working class" [^ch2]

That discipline could not be decreed. It was built through the party's correct leadership and its ties to the masses, tested in "fifteen years of practical history (1903–17)" [^ch2]. Those years took in every form of struggle, from underground circles to mass movements and work in parliament [^ch2].

---

Bolshevism also learned to lose. After the revolution of 1905 was defeated, the revolutionary parties had to retreat. Lenin wrote:

> "victory is impossible unless one has learned how to attack and retreat properly" [^ch3]

Of all the defeated parties, he claimed, the Bolsheviks made "the most orderly retreat" [^ch3]. They did so because they expelled the 'revolutionary phrase-mongers' who would not accept legal work, even in "the most reactionary of parliaments" and "the most reactionary of trade unions" [^ch3].

---

The German 'Lefts' wanted Communists to leave the reactionary trade unions and set up new, clean ones. Lenin called this "ridiculous and childish nonsense" [^ch6]. To leave the unions was to abandon the workers in them to the opportunist leaders. If you want to win the masses, he wrote, you:

> "must absolutely work wherever the masses are to be found" [^ch6]

The task, he insisted, was "to convince the backward elements, to work among them" [^ch6], not to fence oneself off from them with 'Left' slogans.

---

The 'Lefts' also called parliament obsolete. Lenin agreed that it was "historically obsolete" [^ch7] in the long view of world history. But was it politically obsolete for the masses? Millions of German workers were still in favor of parliament [^ch7]. He warned:

> "we must not regard what is obsolete to us as something obsolete to a class, to the masses" [^ch7]

So Communists should stand in elections, and use the parliamentary platform to prove to the backward masses why such parliaments deserve to be done away with [^ch7].

---

Should Communists ever compromise? The 'Lefts' said never. Lenin told a story. Imagine bandits hold up your car. You hand over your money, passport, revolver and car, to get away and catch them later. That is a compromise, but not a betrayal [^ch4].

The peace of Brest-Litovsk with Germany in March 1918 [^tl] was, in Lenin's words, "just that kind of compromise" [^ch4]. The test is what a compromise serves:

> "to reject compromises 'on principle', to reject the permissibility of compromises in general, no matter of what kind, is childishness, which it is difficult even to consider seriously" [^ch4]

---

**The debate:** The Dutch poet and Marxist Herman Gorter answered with an *Open Letter to Comrade Lenin* [^gorterbio]. Western Europe, he argued, was not Russia. There the poor peasants would side with capitalism, and the parties and unions built in parliamentary times opposed every revolutionary action:

> "The workers in Western Europe stand all alone." [^gorter]

Until leaders appeared who would not betray them, Gorter wrote, "we want to do all things from below, and through the dictatorship of the masses themselves" [^gorter]. Lenin had already called this contrast between a dictatorship of the masses and one of the leaders "ridiculously absurd, and stupid" [^ch5].
