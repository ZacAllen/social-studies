The Russian Empire held many nations under the rule of the Tsar. Clause 9 of the Russian Marxists' 1903 program promised them the **right of nations to self-determination**. The Polish Marxist Rosa Luxemburg attacked the clause, and in 1914 Lenin answered her [^ch1].

He refused to settle the question with legal definitions. Capitalism needs a united home market and a common language, so every national movement tends toward a national state [^ch1]. Hence his definition:

> "the self-determination of nations means the political separation of these nations from alien national bodies, and the formation of an independent national state" [^ch1]

---

Lenin's method was to put the question in its concrete historical setting. In Western Europe the age of national revolutions ran from about 1789 to 1871 and was over. But:

> "In Eastern Europe and Asia the period of bourgeois-democratic revolutions did not begin until 1905." [^ch3]

Revolutions in Russia, Persia, Turkey and China were waking whole peoples. Russia itself had a single national center, the roughly 70 million **Great Russians** (ethnic Russians). The "subject peoples" around them made up 57 percent of the population, and were oppressed more harshly than in neighboring states [^ch3].

---

Did backing secession mean backing the oppressed nation's capitalists? Lenin drew a line. Marxists fight oppression in every case, but not the oppressed nation's own drive for privilege:

> "The bourgeois nationalism of any oppressed nation has a general democratic content that is directed against oppression, and it is this content that we unconditionally support" [^ch4]

At the same time, he wrote, "we fight against the tendency of the Polish bourgeois to oppress the Jews" [^ch4].

The deeper point concerned the oppressor nation itself. "Can a nation be free if it oppresses other nations? It cannot." [^ch4] Russian workers could not win their own freedom while their rulers held other peoples down.

---

Russian liberals warned that the right to secede would break up the state. Lenin answered with an everyday comparison:

> "To accuse those who support freedom of self-determination, i. e., freedom to secede, of encouraging separatism, is as foolish and hypocritical as accusing those who advocate freedom of divorce of encouraging the destruction of family ties." [^ch5]

Ordinary people know the value of a big market and a big state. They separate, Lenin argued, only when national oppression makes "joint life absolutely intolerable" [^ch5]. Denying the right only defends the privileges of the dominant nation.

---

Luxemburg had pointed to Norway, which left its forced union with Sweden in August 1905. Lenin turned the example against her. In a referendum about 200,000 Norwegians voted for separation against a few hundred, and Sweden accepted it [^ch6].

Swedish landlords and clergy called for war. The Swedish workers instead defended Norway's right to leave. That, Lenin argued, convinced the Norwegian workers that their Swedish comrades were free of nationalism:

> "The dissolution of the ties imposed upon Norway by the monarchs of Europe and the Swedish aristocracy strengthened the ties between the Norwegian and Swedish workers." [^ch6]

---

Lenin summed up the task of the workers of Russia as twofold. They must fight nationalism of every kind, above all the nationalism of the Great Russians, and recognize every nation's right to secede. And they must keep the workers of all nations in one united organization [^ch10].

His conclusion gave the whole national program in a single line:

> "Complete equality of rights for all nations; the right of nations to self-determination; the unity of the workers of all nations" [^ch10]

---

**The debate:** Luxemburg argued that a general 'right of nations' was an abstraction, because classes divide every nation:

> "In a class society, 'the nation' as a homogeneous socio-political entity does not exist." [^luxnq]

Under capitalism, she wrote, such a right was worth as much as the right "to eat off gold plates" [^luxnq].

Lenin replied that her fear of Polish nationalism made her forget the nationalism of the oppressor. By rejecting the right to secede, "she is in fact assisting the Great-Russian Black Hundreds" [^ch4], the Tsarist far right.
