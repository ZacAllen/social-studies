Marx had seen workers hold power once. In 1871 the workers of Paris took over their city and governed it as the **Paris Commune**. Marx and Engels wrote that one thing especially was proved by the Commune [^ch3]: that "the working class cannot simply lay hold of the ready-made state machinery and wield it for its own purposes" [^ch3].

The Commune's first decree replaced the standing army with the armed people [^ch3]. The old machine was broken up. What would take its place?

---

The Commune's answer looked simple: fuller democracy. Lenin listed its measures:

> "All officials, without exception, elected and subject to recall at any time, their salaries reduced to the level of ordinary 'workmen's wages'" [^ch3]

These measures, he wrote, unite the workers and most of the peasants and "serve as a bridge leading from capitalism to socialism" [^ch3].

Modern capitalism had already simplified most of the work of government into routine registering, filing and checking. Any literate person could do it for a worker's wage, without any "official grandeur" [^ch3].

---

Lenin also took aim at parliament. Under capitalism, he wrote, elections decide only which members of the ruling class will run the state:

> "To decide once every few years which members of the ruling class is to repress and crush the people through parliament--this is the real essence of bourgeois parliamentarism" [^ch3]

His answer was not to abolish elections. Instead the representative institutions must change "from talking shops into 'working' bodies" [^ch3]. In Marx's words, the Commune was "a working, not a parliamentary, body, executive and legislative at the same time" [^ch3].

---

What would the economy look like after the revolution? Lenin followed Marx, who described two phases of communism. In the first, "usually called socialism" [^ch5], the means of production belong to everyone, but each person is still paid according to the work they do.

Its main task is accounting and control by the armed workers themselves. In that phase, Lenin wrote, "The whole of society will have become a single office and a single factory, with equality of labor and pay." [^ch5]

Only in the higher phase can society adopt Marx's rule [^ch5]:

> "From each according to his ability, to each according to his needs" [^ch5]

Then the state can wither away completely.

---

Lenin planned a seventh chapter, on the Russian revolutions of 1905 and 1917, but never wrote it. A political crisis, the eve of the October Revolution, interrupted him [^post]. In November 1917 he added:

> "It is more pleasant and useful to go through the 'experience of revolution' than to write about it." [^post]

Such an interruption, he wrote, "can only be welcomed" [^post].

---

**The debate:** In 1918 Karl Kautsky, the best-known leader of the Second International (the pre-1914 federation of socialist parties) [^pref], attacked Bolshevik rule as dictatorship instead of democracy. He set the rule of a class apart from dictatorship as a form of government:

> "Dictatorship as a form of government means disarming the opposition, by taking from them the franchise, and liberty of the Press and combination." [^kautsky]

Lenin answered that there is no "pure democracy" while classes exist, only the democracy of one class or another. Soviet power took the best buildings and the printing plants from the exploiters and gave them to the working people [^prrk]:

> "Proletarian democracy is a million times more democratic than any bourgeois democracy" [^prrk]
