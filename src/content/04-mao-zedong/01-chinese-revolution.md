In the early twentieth century, China was a **semi-colonial, semi-feudal** country: foreign powers carved out privileges on its coast, while landlords ruled the villages. The Revolution of 1911 ended the Qing dynasty, but left that structure standing [^res].

Then came news of the Russian Revolution of 1917. Mao Zedong, who would become the party's leader, later wrote: "The salvoes of the October Revolution brought us Marxism-Leninism." [^pdd]

The Communist Party of China was founded in 1921 [^pdd]. It began tiny:

> "When the Communist Party of China was founded, it had less than sixty members." [^res]

---

From 1924 the Communists worked inside a **united front** (an alliance of classes against a common enemy) with the **Kuomintang**, the nationalist party then led by Sun Yat-sen [^pdd]. Together they waged the **Northern Expedition** (1924–27) [^res].

In 1927, the Kuomintang under Chiang Kai-shek turned on its allies and "massacred Communists and other revolutionaries" [^res]. The young party was inexperienced, and dominated by what it later called the 'Right capitulationism' of Chen Duxiu. Its membership collapsed from more than 60,000 to a little over 10,000 [^res].

The party's later verdict was blunt: the revolution "suffered a disastrous defeat under the surprise attack of a powerful enemy" [^res].

---

The survivors took up arms. Mao led the **Autumn Harvest Uprising** of 1927 and set up the first rural base area, in the Jinggang Mountains. The Red Army built a central base in Jiangxi province [^res].

Then a second disaster. Under the 'Left' leadership of Wang Ming, the Red Army lost its Jiangxi base in 1934 and set out on the 25,000-li **Long March** to northern Shaanxi (a *li* is a traditional Chinese unit of distance). The Red Army shrank from 300,000 to about 30,000. At Zunyi in January 1935, the leadership turned to Mao [^res].

Mao called the march a victory. It was, he said:

> "a manifesto, a propaganda force, a seeding-machine" [^tactics]

---

Japan was pressing deeper into China. The **Xi'an Incident** of December 1936, organized by the generals Zhang Xueliang and Yang Hucheng, led to renewed cooperation between the two parties against Japan [^res].

In the war of 1937–45, the Red Army was reorganized as the **Eighth Route Army** and the **New Fourth Army**. They fought a guerrilla war behind Japanese lines and set up many anti-Japanese base areas. The Kuomintang, by contrast, "suffered defeat after defeat" [^res].

In 1942 the party launched a **rectification movement**, a campaign of Marxist education that united it around Mao's line [^res].

---

After Japan's defeat, Chiang Kai-shek, backed by the United States, launched an all-out civil war. In the three-year War of Liberation (1946–49), the **People's Liberation Army** destroyed a total of 8,000,000 of Chiang's troops [^res]. The People's Republic of China was founded in October 1949 [^res].

In September 1949, Mao told the delegates gathered to found the new state:

> "Ours will no longer be a nation subject to insult and humiliation. We have stood up." [^stood]

After a century of invasion and civil war, a party of fewer than sixty [^res] had led a quarter of humanity to liberation [^stood].

---

How did they win? Mao's own summary, written in 1949, named three tools: a disciplined Marxist-Leninist party, an army under its leadership, and a united front of all revolutionary classes. "These are the three main weapons with which we have defeated the enemy." [^pdd]

Behind those weapons stood a method. The party had "integrated" Marxism-Leninism "with the concrete practice of the Chinese revolution" [^res]: its theory came from studying China, not from copying Russia.

The lessons that follow read Mao's own works, from his 1927 report on the Hunan peasants to his 1957 speech on contradictions in socialist society.
