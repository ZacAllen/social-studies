---

Before people can do politics, religion, art or philosophy, they have to eat, find shelter and clothe themselves. Marx and Engels made this plain fact the starting point of a method for understanding all of history: **historical materialism**.

Instead of explaining society by the ideas people hold, they explained ideas by the way people produce their lives. As Marx put it in 1859:

> "The mode of production of material life conditions the general process of social, political and intellectual life." [^preface]

This broke with the idealist philosophy of Hegel and his followers, who saw history as the unfolding of ideas or Spirit.

---

Marx described society with an image from architecture. In producing, people enter **relations of production**: who owns the land, the tools and the factories, and who must work for them. Together these form "the economic structure of society, the real foundation, on which arises a legal and political superstructure" [^preface].

Law, the state, religion and philosophy belong to that **superstructure**. They tend to defend the property relations beneath them. In *The German Ideology*, Marx and Engels wrote:

> "The ideas of the ruling class are in every epoch the ruling ideas" [^gi]

---

As the **forces of production** (tools, techniques, skills) develop, they outgrow the old property relations, which become chains on further development. "Then begins an era of social revolution." [^preface]

Classes fight out that conflict. The *Communist Manifesto* opens:

> "The history of all hitherto existing society is the history of class struggles." [^manifesto]

Freeman and slave, lord and serf: every epoch had its oppressors and oppressed, locked in "an uninterrupted, now hidden, now open fight" [^manifesto]. Under capitalism, the *Manifesto* argued, society splits into two great hostile camps: **bourgeoisie** and **proletariat**.

---

Historical materialism does not say that economics mechanically decides everything. After Marx's death, Engels pushed back against followers who read it that way:

> "According to the materialist conception of history, the *ultimately* determining element in history is the production and reproduction of real life. Other than this neither Marx nor I have ever asserted." [^bloch]

Politics, ideas and culture act back on the base. People are not puppets of history either. As Marx wrote in 1852, "Men make their own history, but they do not make it as they please" [^brumaire]. We act, but within conditions inherited from the past.

---

For revolutionaries, historical materialism is a tool, not a museum piece. If social arrangements arise from material conditions, they are not natural or eternal. What human labor built, human labor can rebuild. Capitalism had a beginning, and it can have an end.

That is the meaning of the line carved on Marx's grave in London:

> "The philosophers have only interpreted the world, in various ways; the point is to change it." [^theses]

The traditions that follow in this app all start from this method: study the real conditions, find the contradictions, and organize to transform them.
