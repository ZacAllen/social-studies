In 1875, Germany's two socialist parties prepared to unite at a congress in the town of Gotha. One was the **Eisenach party**, which enemies claimed Marx secretly guided from London. The other followed Ferdinand Lassalle, a socialist agitator whose ideas Marx had long opposed [^bracke] [^fore].

On May 5, 1875, Marx sent sharp marginal notes on the draft program to Wilhelm Bracke, to pass on to the party leaders [^bracke]. He did not oppose unity in action:

> "Every step of real movement is more important than a dozen programmes." [^bracke]

But a program of principles had to be right. Engels published the notes in 1891 [^fore].

---

The draft began: "Labor is the source of all wealth and all culture." [^p1] Marx went straight at it:

> "Labor is not the source of all wealth. Nature is just as much the source of use values (and it is surely of such that material wealth consists!) as labor" [^p1]

Why fuss over a phrase? Because labor always works on nature, a person who owns nothing but their labor power must work for those who own the land and the means of production [^p1]. Socialism has to name the owners, not flatter labor.

---

The program promised workers the "undiminished proceeds of labor" [^p1]. Marx asked what kind of society that could mean. A new society does not appear ready-made:

> "What we have to deal with here is a communist society, not as it has developed on its own foundations, but, on the contrary, just as it emerges from capitalist society" [^p1]

In this **first phase**, after deductions for things such as replacing machinery, administration, schools and health services, each worker gets a certificate for the labor they gave, and draws that much from the common stock [^p1]. Yet people differ in strength and needs, so "This equal right is an unequal right for unequal labor." [^p1]

---

These defects cannot be wished away: "Right can never be higher than the economic structure of society and its cultural development conditioned thereby." [^p1]

Only in a **higher phase**, when the division between mental and physical labor has gone, when work has become "life's prime want", and when production flows abundantly, can society leave the old standard behind and inscribe on its banners: "From each according to his ability, to each according to his needs!" [^p1]

Marx warned against **vulgar socialism**, which treats distribution as separate from production. Change who owns the means of production, and distribution changes with it [^p1].

---

The draft also took up Lassalle's **iron law of wages**: the claim that wages can never rise above a bare minimum. Marx traced this to the population theory of Thomas Malthus, which would make poverty natural and permanent [^p2].

Since Lassalle's death, Marx wrote, the party had learned that wages are a "masked form for the value, or price, of labor power" [^p2], paid only for time also worked unpaid for the capitalist. Returning to Lassalle was like rebel slaves writing:

> "Slavery must be abolished because the feeding of slaves in the system of slavery cannot exceed a certain low maximum!" [^p2]

---

The program demanded a "free state" [^p4]. Marx answered that the workers' aim is not to set the state free: "Freedom consists in converting the state from an organ superimposed upon society into one completely subordinate to it" [^p4].

What state comes after capitalism? Marx answered in two sentences:

> "Between capitalist and communist society there lies the period of the revolutionary transformation of the one into the other. Corresponding to this is also a political transition period in which the state can be nothing but the revolutionary dictatorship of the proletariat." [^p4]

By the **dictatorship of the proletariat** Marx meant what the *Manifesto* had called the working class organized as the ruling class [^cm2].
