On April 1, 1965, Che Guevara wrote a farewell letter to Fidel Castro:

> "I formally resign my positions in the leadership of the party, my post as minister, my rank of commander, and my Cuban citizenship." [^farewell]

He left to fight abroad. In April 1967 the Organization of Solidarity of the Peoples of Africa, Asia and Latin America published his *Message to the Tricontinental* in Havana [^tri]. Guevara himself took the struggle to Bolivia [^tribute]. The message opens with a line from the poet and independence leader José Martí: "Now is the time of the furnaces, and only light should be seen." [^tri]

---

Twenty-one years without a world war, Guevara began, did not mean peace. Korea had been devastated, and in Vietnam the United States now had over half a million troops [^tri].

Vietnam was "tragically alone". Guevara blamed not only US imperialism but also the quarrel between the "two greatest powers of the socialist camp" (the Soviet Union and China) [^tri]. Sympathy from afar was not enough:

> "It is not a matter of wishing success to the victim of aggression, but of sharing his fate; one must accompany him to his death or to victory." [^tri]

---

Guevara surveyed the three continents of the **Tricontinental**: Asia, Africa and Latin America, the main field of imperialist exploitation [^tri]. Each had its own conditions, but in Latin America, under US domination, the local capitalists had lost all power to resist:

> "There are no other alternatives; either a socialist revolution or a make-believe revolution." [^tri]

Armed struggle was already under way in Guatemala, Colombia, Venezuela and Bolivia. Sharing a language, a history and a master, the continent's struggle would become continental [^tri].

---

Guevara's strategy followed from Lenin's idea of imperialism. "We must bear in mind that imperialism is a world system, the last stage of capitalism" [^tri], and it must be defeated in a world confrontation. Its head is the United States.

Vietnam showed how: drag the US army into hostile lands, far from home, until its morale broke. Guevara called for more such fronts:

> "How close we could look into a bright future should two, three or many Vietnams flourish throughout the world" [^tri]

---

Guevara did not promise an easy road. The war would be long and cruel, and it would strike the fighters' families and villages. He demanded hatred of the enemy: "a people without hatred cannot vanquish a brutal enemy." [^tri]

He also demanded **proletarian internationalism** (the duty of workers of every nation to fight for each other's liberation). To die under the flag of Vietnam, Venezuela or Guinea would be as glorious for a Latin American, an Asian or an African as dying for one's own country [^tri].

Differences inside the movement must wait. On the goal, the total destruction of imperialism by armed struggle, there could be no compromise [^tri].

---

The Bolivian campaign ended in defeat. In October 1967 Castro told Cuba that Guevara had been shot after capture, condemning "the shooting against a revolutionary combatant seriously wounded" [^tribute].

The *Message* had already given Guevara's answer to such an end:

> "Wherever death may surprise us, let it be welcome, provided that this, our battle cry, may have reached some receptive ear" [^tri]

Castro insisted the killers were wrong to think they had destroyed his ideas with the man [^tribute].

---

**The debate:** Not all Latin American communists accepted armed struggle. In Venezuela, the Communist Party turned away from its guerrillas toward elections. At the OLAS conference of Latin American revolutionaries in August 1967, Castro denounced its "rightist leadership" and read its reply aloud [^olas]:

> "We are Venezuelan Communists, and we do not accept the tutelage of anyone, no matter how great his revolutionary merits may be." [^olas]

Castro held to Guevara's line: "let no one harbor any illusions about seizing power by peaceful means in any country of this continent." [^olas]
