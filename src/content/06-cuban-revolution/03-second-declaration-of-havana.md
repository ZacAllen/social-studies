Early in 1962, the foreign ministers of the Americas met at Punta del Este, Uruguay, and expelled Cuba from the **Organization of American States** (OAS) [^decl]. On February 4, 1962, Fidel Castro answered before a mass assembly of the Cuban people in Havana. They approved the *Second Declaration of Havana* by a show of hands [^decl].

It opens with José Martí, the hero of Cuba's independence war, writing in 1895 about the United States:

> "I have lived inside the monster and know its guts; and my sling is the sling of David." [^decl]

The Declaration called the OAS "a Yankee Ministry of Colonies" [^decl].

---

The Declaration places Cuba's story inside a world story:

> "What is the history of Cuba but the history of Latin America? And what is the history of Latin America but the history of Asia, Africa, and Oceania?" [^decl]

It retells how capitalism grew into **imperialism**, the stage at which great monopolies export capital and divide the world. Why, then, did the richest power on earth fear a country of seven million? The answer:

> "Not fear of the Cuban Revolution but fear of the Latin American revolution." [^decl]

---

Washington charged that Cuba exports revolution. The Declaration replied: "Revolutions are not exported, they are made by the people." [^decl]

What, then, does Cuba teach? "That revolution is possible, that the people can make it, that in the contemporary world there are no forces capable of halting the liberation movement of the peoples." [^decl]

Cuba could point to its record: it had made landowners of "more than 100,000 small farmers", wiped out illiteracy, and nationalized foreign interests [^decl]. Revolution, the Declaration argued, comes from conditions, and those conditions exist across the continent.

---

The Declaration put numbers to those conditions. On a continent of almost 200 million people, "about four persons per minute die of hunger, of curable illness or premature old age" [^decl]. Meanwhile some $2 billion a year flowed from Latin America to the United States:

> "For each thousand dollars which leave us, there remains one corpse." [^decl]

Against this, the US offered the *Alliance for Progress*, an aid program. The Declaration asked what such an 'alliance' could offer the Indigenous peoples, Black workers and peasants whom imperialism had exploited for centuries [^decl].

---

Who will make the revolution? The working class in Latin America is small, but the peasants, at times over 70% of the population, are a huge potential force if led by the workers and revolutionary intellectuals [^decl].

The **national bourgeoisie** (local capitalists) cannot lead, because it is "paralyzed by fear of social revolution" [^decl]. Yet the front should be broad:

> "There is a place for all progressives, from the old militant Marxist to the sincere Catholic who has nothing to do with the Yankee monopolists and the feudal lords of the land." [^decl]

---

Must the revolution be violent? "Whether this takes place peacefully or in painful birth does not depend on the revolutionaries" [^decl], but on whether the old ruling classes resist. Where they rule by force, "The democratic path is closed completely." [^decl] There it is dishonest to promise change by legal means.

So the Declaration ends in a call to act:

> "The duty of every revolutionary is to make the revolution." [^decl]

Revolutionaries cannot "sit in the doorways of their houses waiting for the corpse of imperialism to pass by" [^decl].

---

**The debate:** Not every Marxist agreed that the democratic path was closed. In Chile, the socialist Salvador Allende won the presidency and set out to build socialism through Congress and the law [^allende]:

> "Today Chile is the first nation on earth to put into practice the second model of transition to a socialist society." [^allende]

Allende trusted the armed forces' tradition of obedience to civil authority [^allende]. On September 11, 1973, they overthrew him in a US-backed coup, and he died in the presidential palace [^allende73]. His last broadcast promised: "I will pay for loyalty to the people with my life." [^allende73]
