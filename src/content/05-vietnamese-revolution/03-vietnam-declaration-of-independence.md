On September 2, 1945, at Ba Dinh Square in Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh read a Declaration of Independence on behalf of the Provisional Government, before a crowd the Party put at nearly a million [^cpv]. Japan had just surrendered, and the people had risen to found the **Democratic Republic of Vietnam** [^decl].

Ho began with the words of another revolution:

> "All men are created equal. They are endowed by their Creator with certain inalienable rights, among these are Life, Liberty, and the pursuit of Happiness." [^decl]

The American original of 1776 reads "unalienable Rights" [^us]. The Declaration called it an "immortal statement" [^decl].

---

Ho then drew out what the statement meant for a colony:

> "In a broader sense, this means: All the peoples on the earth are equal from birth, all the peoples have a right to live, to be happy and free." [^decl]

He added the French Revolution's Declaration of the Rights of Man, which he dated to 1791 [^decl]. (It was adopted in 1789, and its first article reads "Men are born and remain free and equal in rights." [^rights]) Then came the turn. For more than eighty years the French had violated Vietnam, "abusing the standard of Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity" [^decl]. France was judged by its own creed.

---

The case against France followed, charge by charge. In politics, the French had denied every democratic liberty, and split Vietnam into three regimes, North, Center and South, to wreck its unity [^decl].

> "They have built more prisons than schools." [^decl]

They had forced opium and alcohol on the people. In economics, they had seized the rice fields, mines and forests, monopolized banknotes and the export trade, and taxed the peasantry into extreme poverty. They had even held back the national bourgeoisie, the native capitalist class [^decl]. Every class had a grievance.

---

In the autumn of 1940, Ho went on, the French had handed the country to Japan, and the people bore "the double yoke of the French and the Japanese" [^decl]. In the famine that followed, from the end of 1944 into 1945, "more than two million of our fellow-citizens died from starvation" [^decl].

On March 9, 1945, the Japanese disarmed the French, who fled or surrendered [^decl]. So France had no claim to return:

> "The truth is that we have wrested our independence from the Japanese and not from the French." [^decl]

---

The Declaration announced a revolution, not only a new flag. The last emperor, Bao Dai, had abdicated [^decl].

> "Our people at the same time have overthrown the monarchic regime that has reigned supreme for dozens of centuries." [^decl]

In its place stood a Democratic Republic. Its Provisional Government broke off "all relations of a colonial character with France", repealed the treaties France had signed in Vietnam's name, and abolished the special rights the French had held [^decl].

---

Ho closed by holding the Allies to their word. At Tehran and San Francisco they had backed self-determination, so they could not refuse to recognize Vietnam [^decl]. He declared that Vietnam "has the right to be a free and independent country and in fact it is so already" [^decl].

Three weeks later, on September 23, 1945, French troops, backed by British bayonets, seized Saigon [^oneyear]. The Declaration became the charter of the long war that followed: a colonized people claiming, in the colonizers' own language, the rights the colonizers proclaimed.
