In October 1966, in Oakland, California, **Huey P. Newton** and **Bobby Seale** founded the Black Panther Party for Self-Defense [^mia]. Its founding document, dated October 15, 1966, was the **Ten-Point Program** [^tpp].

Each point has two parts: a short demand beginning 'We want', then a paragraph beginning 'We believe' that gives the reasons. The first point sets the theme for all ten:

> "We believe that Black people will not be free until we are able to determine our destiny." [^tpp]

This is **self-determination**: the right of an oppressed people to decide its own future instead of having it decided for them.

---

Points two to four are about wealth. Point two demanded full employment and held the federal government responsible for giving every man a job or a guaranteed income. If business would not do it:

> "the means of production should be taken from the businessmen and placed in the community" [^tpp]

The **means of production** are the land, factories and tools that wealth is made with. Point three demanded the "overdue debt of forty acres and two mules", promised a century earlier as restitution for slavery [^tpp]. Point four said that if landlords would not provide decent housing, the land and housing should become **cooperatives**, owned and run by the people who live there [^tpp].

---

Point five demanded an education that "exposes the true nature of this decadent American society" and teaches Black people their true history. Without knowledge of himself and his place in society, the Program argued, a man has little chance to relate to anything else [^tpp].

Point six demanded that Black men be exempt from military service. The Program refused to defend a government that did not protect Black people, and looked beyond America's borders:

> "We will not fight and kill other people of color in the world who, like Black people, are being victimized by the White racist government of America." [^tpp]

Black Americans and the oppressed peoples abroad, in this view, faced the same enemy.

---

Point seven made the Panthers famous. It demanded an immediate end to police brutality, and its answer was organized self-defense:

> "We believe we can end police brutality in our Black community by organizing Black self-defense groups that are dedicated to defending our Black community from racist police oppression and brutality." [^tpp]

It cited the Second Amendment's right to bear arms [^tpp]. Newton and Seale put this into practice by "patrolling the pigs": following the police through Black neighborhoods, armed and within the law, to see that people's rights were respected [^carson]. Newton gave the reason in 1967: "An unarmed people are slaves or are subject to slavery at any given moment." [^dsd]

---

Points eight and nine turned to the courts. Point eight demanded freedom for all Black prisoners, because they "have not received a fair and impartial trial" [^tpp].

Point nine demanded that Black defendants be tried by juries from their own communities. The Program read the Fourteenth Amendment as promising trial by one's peers, and defined a peer as:

> "a person from a similar economic, social, religious, geographical, environmental, historical and racial background" [^tpp]

Black defendants, it said, had been tried by "all-White juries that have no understanding of the 'average reasoning man' of the Black community" [^tpp].

---

Point ten summed up the whole: "We Want Land, Bread, Housing, Education, Clothing, Justice And Peace." It closed by quoting the Declaration of Independence (1776): when a government destroys the people's rights, they may "alter or abolish it" [^tpp].

In 1970 Newton explained what kind of document this was [^boston]:

> "A Ten‐Point Program is not revolutionary in itself, nor is it reformist. It is a survival program." [^boston]

It would keep the people alive and organized until a total transformation was possible. The Program kept growing with the Party's work. After the leadership called for free clinics, a new point added in 1972 demanded "completely free health care for all Black and oppressed people" [^bassett].
