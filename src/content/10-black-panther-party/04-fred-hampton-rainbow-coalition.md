In 1968 the Black Panther Party set up an Illinois chapter on the West Side of Chicago. It soon came under the leadership of **Fred Hampton**, a young civil rights organizer known for his powerful speaking [^newberry].

As chairman, Hampton led five breakfast programs on the West Side, helped create a free medical center, and began door-to-door testing for **sickle cell anemia** (an inherited blood disease common among Black Americans). The chapter also reached out to Chicago's street gangs to draw them away from crime and into the class struggle [^mia].

He was killed by police on December 4, 1969, at the age of 21 [^mia]. In about a year he built a movement that is still studied.

---

Hampton taught the Panthers' method of education. People learn, he said, by "observation and participation": they see a thing done, then take part in it [^power]. Theory without practice was worth nothing:

> "I don't care how much theory you got, if it don't have any practice applied to it, then that theory happens to be irrelevant." [^power]

The breakfast program was his favorite example. People who were frightened of the word 'socialism' still defended a socialist program that fed their children. "A lot of people think it is charity, but what does it do? It takes the people from a stage to another stage." [^power]

---

Hampton insisted that the struggle was one of class. "We say primarily that the priority of this struggle is class," he told students in November 1969 [^classs]. The poor came in every color, and so did the exploiter. Of the capitalist who exploits the community, he said: "Sometimes he's black and sometimes he's white." [^power]

That ruled out answering racism with more racism, or capitalism with Black-owned business:

> "We say you don't fight capitalism with no black capitalism; you fight capitalism with socialism." [^power]

His shorter slogan for the same idea was that the Panthers would "fight racism with solidarity" [^power].

---

Hampton put that principle into practice. Early in 1969 the **Young Lords**, a mostly Puerto Rican street gang in Lincoln Park turned political organization under **José 'Cha Cha' Jiménez**, occupied a Chicago police station to protest police violence. Hampton read the news and sought out Jiménez [^newberry] [^ssw].

Panther organizer **Bob Lee** then built an alliance with the **Young Patriots**: poor white Southerners and Appalachian migrants in the Uptown neighborhood, who called themselves "dislocated hillbillies" [^newberry]. With Hampton, Jiménez and William 'Preacherman' Fesperman of the Young Patriots as its leaders, the **Rainbow Coalition** was formed in 1969: a multiracial alliance of working-class organizations [^newberry].

---

The coalition's rule was to organize where you live, then unite. Billy 'Che' Brooks, an Illinois Panther, recalled:

> "Our thing was that Black people organize in the Black community, Puerto Ricans organize in the Puerto Rican community, 'mexicanos' organize in the Mexican community, and poor white people organize in their community" [^ssw]

The Young Lords and Young Patriots copied the Panther survival programs: free breakfasts, daycare, and a Young Patriots health clinic [^ssw] [^newberry]. Trust came slowly: the Young Patriots wore the Confederate flag as their emblem [^ssw].

Repression followed. In May 1969 the mayor and the county's chief prosecutor declared a 'war on gangs' and put 1,000 more police on the streets [^ssw].

---

**The debate:** The **Progressive Labor Party** (PL), a rival party on the left, attacked the Panthers. In February 1969 its magazine said the Panthers "have no class outlook". It said of the breakfast program: "we feel it isn't a forward step but a backward one." [^davidson]

Hampton answered in April 1969 that people cannot reach communism by abstract argument. You crawl before you walk:

> "the Black Panther Party, as the vanguard party, thought that the Breakfast for Children Program was the best technique of crawling that any vanguard party could follow." [^liberator]

At its 1969 national convention, Students for a Democratic Society (SDS) expelled PL. Its attack on the Panthers was one of the charges [^davidson].
