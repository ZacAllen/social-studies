**Huey P. Newton** (1942–1989) met **Bobby Seale** as a fellow student at Merritt College in Oakland, and in October 1966 the two founded the Black Panther Party [^carson]. He was its Minister of Defense [^mia], and his ideas held the Party together [^carson].

On October 28, 1967, Newton was arrested after a clash with Oakland police in which one officer died. In September 1968 he was convicted of voluntary manslaughter. A national 'Free Huey' campaign followed, the conviction was reversed on appeal in 1970, and he was released [^carson]. He credited the movement:

> "I would be in a maximum‐security penitentiary if it were not for the power of the people." [^boston]

This lesson follows two of his ideas: **revolutionary suicide** and **intercommunalism**.

---

In 1967 Newton set out the job of the **vanguard party**: the organized, most committed part of the people, which leads by example. The people, he argued, learn mostly by watching and taking part, so the party must teach "through literature and activities" [^correct].

That role was dangerous, and Newton faced the danger openly:

> "A revolutionary must realize that if he is sincere, death is imminent due to the fact that the things he is saying and doing are extremely dangerous." [^correct]

Without this realization, he wrote, it was impossible to proceed as a revolutionary [^correct]. Here was the seed of an idea he would develop in prison.

---

In solitary confinement, Newton read of a study finding that the suicide rate among young Black men had doubled in ten to fifteen years. He recalled the sociologist Émile Durkheim, who traced suicide to social conditions, not individual temperament [^rs].

He called this **reactionary suicide**: "the reaction of a man who takes his own life in response to social conditions that overwhelm him and condemn him to helplessness" [^rs].

Newton saw a worse form too: a death of the spirit, in people who had stopped fighting their oppression because resistance seemed hopeless [^rs]. As he told a Boston audience, anyone who fails to preserve himself under these conditions dies a suicide, "reactionary suicide because reactionary conditions will have caused his death" [^boston].

---

**Revolutionary suicide** is the opposite choice: to oppose the forces that would drive a person to self-murder, even at the risk of death, because only resistance can change intolerable conditions [^rs].

> "Revolutionary suicide does not mean that I and my comrades have a death wish; it means just the opposite." [^rs]

The desire to live with hope and dignity is so strong, Newton explained, that life without them is impossible [^rs]. He put it plainly in 1970:

> "Of course, our real desire is to live, but we will not be cowed, we will not be intimidated." [^boston]

---

At Boston College in November 1970, Newton traced how the Party's ideas had changed. In 1966 the Panthers were Black nationalists. Then they became revolutionary nationalists ('nationalism plus socialism'), then internationalists [^boston].

But the United States had become an empire, Newton argued, and nations under its control had lost their economic, cultural and political independence. So:

> "These transformations and phenomena require us to call ourselves 'intercommunalists' because nations have been transformed into communities of the world." [^boston]

He presented the change as self-criticism: "the Black Panther Party is not embarrassed to change or admit error" [^boston].

---

Newton called the existing world order **reactionary intercommunalism**: an imperial ruling circle laying siege to every community on earth, so that its institutions no longer served its own people [^boston].

The Party's goal was **revolutionary intercommunalism**:

> "This would be the time when the people seize the means of production and distribute the wealth and the technology in an egalitarian way to the many communities of the world." [^boston]

Because the empire had stolen from the whole world, Newton argued, a revolution only in North America could not be enough. Communism, a world without the state, would come after a period of revolutionary intercommunalism [^boston].

---

Newton's own life ended in the condition he had warned against. In the 1970s he faced published charges of extortion and assault against other Black people, fled to Cuba to escape criminal charges, and later became involved in the drug trade [^carson].

On August 22, 1989, he was shot dead on an Oakland street in a drug dispute [^mia]. The Marxist Internet Archive's history of the Party judged him by his own concept: he had been "killing himself by reactionary suicide, through the vices of drug addiction" [^mia].

His ideas outlived him: resistance as the choice of life, and a revolution that must be worldwide.
