# Lessons teach sympathetically, and criticise from within the tradition

The curriculum exists to bring readers to Marxism, not to survey opinions about it. So each lesson teaches its thinker or movement sympathetically and on their own terms. Debates are drawn from within the socialist tradition (Luxemburg answering Lenin, say). Setbacks and errors, such as the Great Leap Forward famine, are faced directly, from a Marxist standpoint of criticism and self-criticism. We considered a neutral academic survey that includes liberal and anti-communist critiques, and rejected it because it doesn't serve the curriculum's purpose. We considered straight advocacy that leaves setbacks out, and rejected it because a reader who meets those setbacks elsewhere first stops trusting the app. In every case the stance governs framing, never accuracy: every factual claim is cited, and every quote is checked.
